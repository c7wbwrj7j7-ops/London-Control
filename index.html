Automatic feed (beta)

Without the feed you add aircraft by hand at the top of Fly: callsign, fix and time (or +1 … +6 min). That always works. The feed is a helper on top: it reads the game clock and the Tactical and Hold Lists from your PC and adds and updates the aircraft for you. You can switch it off at any moment; your aircraft stay.

Guide: switch on the feed

  1. PC: open the folder Documents\LondonControlFeed and double-click START.bat. The first time it installs what it needs (a few minutes). Leave the black window open.
  2. Game: start London Control. Keep the clock panel, the Tactical List and the Hold List open. Make the lists tall enough to show all rows (they may be behind other windows).
  3. iPad: same Wi-Fi as the PC. Scan the QR code in the black window, or type the address it shows (like http://192.168.178.59:8765). Add it to the home screen if you like.
  4. On that page tap Auto feed (top right in Fly) and then Turn auto feed ON.
  5. Check: the clock follows the game within a few seconds, and aircraft from the Tactical List appear. In Setup, “Correction on typed fix time” should be 0:00.
  6. Something wrong? Turn the feed off and add by hand. On the PC, the file status.txt in the same folder shows what the helper reads; tell Claude “check”.

The helper only reads the screen. It never changes the game or its files.

1. Quick start
  • Set the route: at the bottom, in the settings per fix: the shortest route from each fix to the merge point (for example 12:00). This is the shortest way at 250 kt.
  • Sim time: enter the simulator time in Sim time. The app never uses the clock of the device. Aircraft that are past the merge are deleted. The last one stays as leader, so the separation behind it is correct.
  • Add an aircraft: callsign, category, fix and the estimated time over the fix. If you enter the same callsign again, it is updated (the first ETA is kept).
  • During the session: enter ato when an aircraft is over the fix, and act when it is over the merge. The app learns from this and builds statistics.
  • All times are sim time in HH:MM or HH:MM:SS. Data stays only on this device (an iPad and a PC each have their own data).

3. How the order is decided
  • The base is FIFO on ETA merge. An aircraft may move forward or back by at most Max shift places, if this makes the total delay smaller.
  • Separation: the 6-category wake table (J, H, U, M, S, L) in nm, changed to seconds with the final speed (default 160 kt), plus the Buffer (default 20 s). A Super as following aircraft needs 3 nm behind every category, except behind a Small or Light (slow on final): 4.5 nm. Every aircraft keeps its own separation to the one ahead, so the wake rules stay in place for all other categories.
  • Solving delay: first with vectors or speed (+Extra per fix, default 3:00). If more is needed, with complete hold circuits (default 5:00). Default routes from fix to merge: BNN 8:00, LAM 5:00, BIG 4:00, OCK 5:00. Flying faster (-Faster, default 1:00) is used only if it helps an aircraft behind.
  • Fairness: an aircraft that has already flown circuits counts more. Max holds (default 2) limits the number of circuits per aircraft, but not strictly: the app gives a warning if it becomes more. Fairness (default 600) gives a penalty for a big delay for one aircraft; a lower number is stricter, 0 = off.
  • Fixing: when an aircraft is over the fix (ato or sim time), its hold is fixed. An aircraft in the hold stays at least the circuits that are already flown, and an aircraft that is out of the hold is never put back in a hold. If the EAT is past, the merge time is fixed (COMMIT).
  • Prio gives an aircraft its own ETA (for example a medical aircraft, or a Super that waited a long time); the others are planned around it. Lock keeps the current merge time of an aircraft. If not all of this is possible, a warning is shown and they are ignored for the moment.
4. Suggestion and adopting it
  • Your current sequence does not change by itself. The app also calculates a suggestion with more freedom (Shift suggestion, default 10).
  • If the suggestion is better, you see the column Suggestion and a block with the order, the gain in total delay and the action for each aircraft.
  • Adopt fixes the suggestion (LOCK). Auto order or unlock releases it again.
  • The suggestion is calculated only for a maximum of 11 free aircraft.
5. Holding, EAT and EFC
  • EAT = expected departure from the hold according to the plan: time over the fix plus complete circuits.
  • EFC (expected further clearance) = the moment you release the aircraft from the hold towards the merge. By default this is the EAT. You can fill in the EFC yourself in the EFC column (HH:MM:SS); a ✎ shows that it is manual. Make it empty = automatic again.
  • With a manual EFC, the time in the hold is exact (no longer in complete circuits). After that, the aircraft flies the route, so the earliest merge is EFC + route. Everything behind it is planned on this automatically.
  • Unable hold: the aircraft cannot hold (any more). The plan then solves the delay with vectors or puts others behind it.
  • Out of the hold: when an aircraft leaves the hold, you enter a time again in Actual fix (same field). The first ETA and the first ato are kept.
6. How the app decides the number of holds

You do not need to count the circuits. The app chooses in this order:

  1. typed: the number you enter in Holds (always wins).
  2. ato2: measured from the second ato (out of hold minus first ato, divided by the circuit time). This is the most reliable source.
  3. deriv (derived): from act − ato − route.
  4. plan: the number with which the hold was fixed.

A ? after the source means that the derived number is uncertain. Such an aircraft is not used in the learned route, until you type the number.

7. Events and randomizer
  • Fuel: maximum delay in minutes; the app gives a warning if the plan is more than this. Medical: gets priority (prio). Long final: extra seconds of separation behind this aircraft. Unable hold and unable to fly faster.
  • Random: the app sometimes gives an event to a random aircraft (you can set it: every N actions, chance in %). This is useful for practice. Event now forces one immediately.
  • The log with events is below the card.
8. Statistics and score
  • Over the fix: actual minus first ETA. How good was the estimate?
  • Over the merge: actual minus planned. How correct was the plan?
  • Fix to merge without hold: the real travel time. The hold time is taken away (act − ato2, or act − ato − circuits). This is what the route really costs; Avg extra is the difference with the route you set.
  • Learned route: with Average route = on, the app uses for each fix the average of your own observations instead of the route you set (at least Min. observations). See the line "Route used".
  • No stat: excludes an aircraft from statistics, score and learned routes (useful when you improvise).
  • Score (0–100): Accuracy 30 (actual vs planned, ±15 s), Efficiency 25 (average loss), Fairness 15 (maximum loss, up to 3 min free), Light solutions 15 (a hold counts 1, a 360 counts ½), Tight arrivals 15 (few gaps). Gaps smaller than the separation are only reported, not scored.

10. Tips for busy moments
  • The minimum: ato at the fix, ato again when leaving the hold, act at the merge. The app calculates the rest.
  • An aircraft that is always put at the back (for example a Super)? Set prio, or lower Max holds / Fairness.
  • Same fix, fixed order: aircraft that are already over the same fix are never overtaken by an aircraft that came over that fix later, except if the first one is in the hold. Events (fuel, medical, long final) and prio are still excepted.
  • Random events only happen to aircraft that have not reached their fix yet. Aircraft that are over the fix (on their way to the merge, or in the hold) are never picked, since an urgent event there makes no sense. You can still set an event by hand on any aircraft.
  • Slot kept at the ato: when you enter the first ato, the merge time that was planned for the aircraft stays (the sequence does not shuffle). The app only recalculates how much hold or vectoring is still needed, so an aircraft that already absorbed delay on the way to the fix gets less hold but the same slot. The message shows it, for example "Slot kept: merge 10:21:10, hold 1x → none". If the slot is no longer possible (for example he arrives much later), the plan adjusts and says so.
  • Forgot an ato? If you enter an ato that lies in the past (more than 30 s before the sim time), the app assumes he has already passed the fix without holding and does not give him a hold. If he is in fact holding, press in hold.
  • Totals: under the score you see the total delay (sum of the delay of every finished aircraft compared to its first ETA), the missed time and the empty time. Missed time is the time the runway was free while an aircraft was already over its fix long enough and could have been there, but was still waiting in the hold or on vectors. Empty time is runway time that was free because no aircraft was available yet, so it is not counted against you. Runway use is the minimum separations as a share of the time between the first and last arrival. The three largest missed gaps are listed with the callsigns.
  • Learning extra time: besides the average route, the app learns how much extra time the flights from each fix really absorbed (the upper quarter of the observed fix-to-merge times). If that is more than the extra you set, the larger value is used for planning (shown as "Extra used … learned"). It never goes below what you set. Set Min. observations to 3 or more so one odd flight does not decide.
  • Cleaner table: the Gap, EFC and Suggestion columns are gone. The suggestion is a single banner above the table (Apply / Ignore, with details), the EFC can be typed on the holding card, and the Holds field only appears when the app is unsure or after you typed a number. Time fields open the number keypad: type 1412 for 14:12 or 141230 for 14:12:30 (a colon still works).
  • Holding board: aircraft in the hold get a card above the sequence: circuits flown (dots), the next pass over the fix and three buttons. Out now releases at the sim time, Out at pass at the next pass over the fix (both set the EFC), and Direct, no hold cancels the hold for an aircraft you send straight on. Under the buttons you see what each choice would do: the new merge time, and the gap to the aircraft ahead and behind (✓ = at or above the minimum, ⚠ = below). Nothing else to administer: enter the real times later if you like.
  • One-tap ✓: the ✓ next to ato enters the estimated time over the fix, and the ✓ next to act enters the planned merge time. Use it for aircraft that are on schedule and type only the exceptions.
  • En route time and holds flown: as soon as an aircraft has an actual merge time, its row shows how long it was on its way from the fix to the merge, split in time in the hold and the free route. If the number of holds is not clear from the times, the app asks how many holds the aircraft flew (OK with a number confirms it, empty accepts the guess, Cancel leaves it unsure with a ?).
  • End session: the End session button builds the end-of-session screen. At the top are the values for next time (route fix to merge, extra, faster and hold circuit per fix, plus the general settings) with the advice to take a screenshot, then the score with stars and a rating, and then the full report: every aircraft with times, holds, en route time, total loss and separation to the aircraft ahead (red when below minimum), then the exceptions (below minimum separation, more than ±tolerance off plan, more holds than the maximum, total loss over 10 minutes, medical and fuel results), the aircraft not finished or excluded, the events, and the full statistics and score. Print / PDF makes a copy to keep. It does not delete anything, so you can keep working and press it again.
  • Emergencies stand out: an aircraft with medical or fuel gets a red row with a red frame and a red badge under its callsign. Other events (long final, no hold, no fast) get an amber row, and prio a blue badge. The callsign column stays fixed on the left when you swipe the table sideways.
  • Still in hold: an aircraft that is put in the hold stays in it (status STILL IN HOLD, always the next circuit as the earliest exit) until you enter its second actual fix time or an EFC. So even if the expected circuits are finished, it does not move into the sequence without notice. With the button in hold you put an aircraft in the hold yourself (and out again).
  • Pop-up for illogical times: if you enter a time that would make the statistics wrong, a warning is shown. This happens for: ato more than 10 min earlier or 15 min later than the first ETA; 2nd ato less than 2 min after the first (no hold); a time on or after the act; act not later than the ato; fix→merge faster than 60% of the route; or a separation to the neighbour aircraft that is well below the minimum. Choose OK to save anyway, or Cancel and then choose if you save the aircraft but exclude it from statistics (no stat), or save nothing.
  • If you improvise with an aircraft, set no stat on so that your statistics stay correct.
  • Did you change something by mistake? Undo reverses the last change.
  • Space shows gaps where one more aircraft fits and the first free slot behind the last one.
  • For a big gap in the hold: look at Suggestion and think about an earlier EFC for an aircraft in the hold.

Settings per fix


How the delay of an aircraft is taken away:

  1. Up to Extra: slow down and vectors. Label: SLOW.
    2. 

  2. More than Extra: one or more 360s after the fix (time per turn below, maximum set in "Max 360s"), plus speed for the rest. Label: 360 or 2×360.
    3. 

  3. More than that: holding. The aircraft leaves the hold at its EFC, at any moment (vectored out), then uses Extra for the rest. Label: HOLD (number of circuits started).

Faster is how much time an aircraft can win by flying faster. Label: FAST.

Type times in minutes (3) or minutes:seconds (2:30).
Speed before the fix: tap the callsign on a label and choose a speed (220–340). The time over the fix is changed with the ratio to the normal speed (set below), from the moment you tap. Aircraft with a speed change are not used to learn Appear → fix.
Appear → fix: if you add an aircraft without a time, its time over the fix is now + this time (shown with ~). It is learned per stack from your "fix now" taps after 3 aircraft (with Learn route time on).
Stack levels: lowest and highest holding level, 1000 ft apart (80 – 120 = 5 levels). Aircraft in the hold get a level on their label, the first to leave at the bottom. In a stack the lowest aircraft (first in) leaves first. When a stack is full or nearly full, its aircraft get priority in the landing order, so it empties first. You get a warning if it is still full.
